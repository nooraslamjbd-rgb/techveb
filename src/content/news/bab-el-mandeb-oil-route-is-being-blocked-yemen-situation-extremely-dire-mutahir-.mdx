Saudi Arabia is Yemen’s most important and largest partner, while the Yemeni government is trying to restore its presence across the country, Yemen’s ambassador to Pakistan told journalists.

ISLAMABAD, (UrduPoint / Pakistan Point News - 12th Sep, 2026) [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html)’s Ambassador to [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/pakistan/) Muhammad Mutahir Al-Ashabi said the situation in [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/arabic/yemen.html) was extremely dire, adding that Bab el-Mandeb, an international [oil](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/oil.html) passage, was being blocked. Speaking to journalists in [Islamabad](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/islamabad.html), the Yemeni ambassador said [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html) and [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/pakistan.html) had highly positive relations, with nearly 500 Yemeni students studying in [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/pakistan.html).

He said [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html) was currently facing extremely difficult challenges, the biggest of which concerned the country’s integrity and sovereignty. Ambassador Muhammad Mutahir Al-Ashabi said Bab el-Mandeb was a global and highly important route for energy, [oil](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/oil.html) and [food](https://www.urdupoint.com/cooking/).

He said 60 percent of [oil](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/oil.html) passed through Bab el-Mandeb, and its closure would create a global energy challenge. He added that peace was the Yemeni [government](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/government.html)’s priority. The Yemeni ambassador said the Houthis had their own agenda and wanted to gain power and turn other Yemenis into second-class citizens.

He said the Yemeni [government](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/government.html) wanted lasting peace, while the Houthis were refusing negotiations and had increased tensions in the current situation. Muhammad Mutahir Al-Ashabi said [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/pakistan.html) was an important country that could do—and was doing—a great deal.

He said an international [agreement](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/agreement.html) had been reached with [Saudi Arabia](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/saudi-arabia.html) regarding [Najran](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/najran.html), Jazan and several other southern areas, and the matters had been resolved through negotiations and under the international [agreement](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/agreement.html). He said [Saudi Arabia](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/saudi-arabia.html) was [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html)’s most important and largest partner, adding that the Yemeni [government](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/government.html) was trying to restore its presence in the country.

He said his [government](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/government.html) was attempting to unite other groups against the Houthis. Ambassador Muhammad Mutahir Al-Ashabi said [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html) had large reserves of [oil](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/oil.html) and [gas](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/gas.html) as well as other natural resources.

He said weapons were reaching the Houthis by sea routes, while the [United States](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/united-states.html) was merely watching the situation [from](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/from.html) outside. He said the Houthis relied on ballistic missiles and drones. He added that [Yemen](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/yemen.html) was importing pharmaceutical products [from](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/from.html) [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/en/latest-news/pakistan.html) and was implementing the agreements concluded by former President Ali Abdullah Saleh.
