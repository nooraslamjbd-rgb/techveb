## Himalayan Glacier Collapse Triggers Fatal Floods

Preliminary scientific investigations reveal that a massive glacial collapse caused the devastating flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border. According to reports from BBC News, the catastrophe resulted in at least 389 fatalities and widespread infrastructure destruction.

Initial reports suggested an earthquake caused a landslide. However, the US Geological Survey (USGS) later clarified that a disintegration of glacial ice triggered the event. The physical impact of the ice hitting the valley floor generated a seismic shockwave measuring 5.2 magnitude.

### Scientific Analysis and River Surge

Experts from the University of Dundee and the International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development (ICIMOD) analyzed the disaster site near Langtang Lirung peak. Their research indicates that an ice-rock avalanche swept into Lende Khola, a key tributary of the Bhote Koshi river.

Key impact metrics include:
- **Rapid Surge:** Water levels downstream surged by 7 to 9 meters within 30 minutes.
- **Station Destruction:** Multiple hydrological monitoring stations were completely washed away.
- **Mass Debris Flow:** A sudden torrent carried sediment, ice, and massive boulders downstream.

### Growing Climate Risks in the Himalayas

This disaster highlights the critical danger of rapidly melting Himalayan ice sheets. Scientists warn that rising global temperatures are accelerating glacial instability, creating severe hazards for high-altitude communities across Asia.
