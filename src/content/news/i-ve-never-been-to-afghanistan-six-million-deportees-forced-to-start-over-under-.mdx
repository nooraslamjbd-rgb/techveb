## The Massive Surge in Afghan Deportations

In what relief agencies describe as one of the largest cross-border movements of people globally, over six million Afghans have returned to their homeland in under three years. Driven out by aggressive border crackdowns in Pakistan and Iran, millions of refugees are forced to restart their lives in an economically strained Afghanistan.

### Unprecedented Displacement Across Borders

For decades, millions of Afghans sought safety and livelihoods in neighboring nations. However, recent policy shifts by Pakistan and Iran have led to mass expulsions targeting both undocumented migrants and legal permit holders.

- **6 Million Returnees:** Equal to the population of a small country returning since August 2021.
- **Crackdown Scope:** Includes individuals carrying official Proof of Registration (PoR) cards.
- **Second-Generation Deportees:** Many returning individuals were born abroad and have never previously visited Afghanistan.

## Life Under Taliban Rule and Growing Crises

As returnees cross border points like Torkham, they encounter severe humanitarian challenges. According to reporting by BBC News, families arrive facing acute shortages of food, shelter, and basic resources.

Human rights groups and the UN Refugee Agency (UNHCR) continue to raise alarms regarding the speed and scale of these deportations. With no sign of the expulsions slowing down, Afghanistan's fragile infrastructure remains under overwhelming pressure to support millions of vulnerable citizens.
