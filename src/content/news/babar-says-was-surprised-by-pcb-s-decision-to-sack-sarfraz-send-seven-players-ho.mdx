The Pakistan Cricket Board’s (PCB) [decision](https://www.dawn.com/news/2026428) to sack the national team’s head coach and send seven players back home following the defeat against England at Lord’s last week came as a surprise to captain Babar Azam, he revealed on Tuesday.

The [194-run drubbing](https://www.dawn.com/news/2026161) — which sealed the three-match series for the hosts — was followed by Sarfraz Ahmed’s removal as Pakistan’s head coach and Umar Gul’s dismissal as bowling coach. They were replaced by white-ball head coach Mike Hesson and Ashley Noffke, respectively.

Seven players, including seasoned campaigners Imam-ul-Haq, Mohammad Rizwan and Salman Ali Agha, were axed from the squad midway through the series, while five of the replacements flown to the UK were uncapped players.

The wholesale changes, as it emerged later, were planned outside the Pakistan dressing room by the national selection panel. As was the case with most members of the team’s contingent, the developments came as a surprise to Babar as well.

“It was news to me as well,” Babar said during the pre-match press conference ahead of the final Test at Edgbaston. “I only found out about these things later.”

Following the PCB’s dramatic move, the board’s high-performance director and selector [Aaqib Javed](https://www.dawn.com/news/2026783) revealed that the decision to sack the coaching staff was a reaction to their inability to make certain selection calls ahead of the second Test.

While Sarfraz and Umar bore the brunt of the PCB’s action, Babar was retained to lead the side in the final Test. The captain, however, distanced himself from the board’s decisions.

Pakistan’s Azan Awais during practice ahead of the third Test match against England at Edgbaston, Birmingham, England on September 7, 2026. — Reuters

“But as I said, the PCB made the decisions, so I think they would be better placed to explain them,” he said. “Whatever decision the PCB has taken, as a player and as captain, I have to go along with the decision made by the cricket board.

“…The PCB would have made its decision after giving it considerable thought.

“In my opinion, it would be better to ask them about it because they would be able to explain it much better.”

Babar also gave a straight “no” when asked whether he was aware of the PCB’s internal inquiry into the discipline and conduct of certain players.

## Nothing to lose

Babar and his players have little option but to put a tumultuous week behind them and focus on what they can produce on the field. To do so, however, he and Hesson have a number of fresh faces to choose from.

The uncapped duo of pacer Razaullah and all-rounder Arafat Minhas could make their debuts, while Saim Ayub — who has eight Tests to his name — is likely to return to the side.

Babar described the situation as an opportunity for the young players amid the chaos.

England’s Jamie Smith during practice ahead of the third Test match against England at Edgbaston, Birmingham, England on September 7, 2026. — Reuters

“…The new players are coming in, and they have a golden opportunity to grab it and play this game,” he said, adding that the final Test, being a dead rubber, could help Pakistan handle the pressure better.

“For us, this is an opportunity as a team and as a group. The series is lost, but we have nothing to lose. So we can play freely, go out onto the ground and believe in ourselves as a team,” he added.

“We have had a couple of days of practice and have spoken to each other a lot. Whenever a new team or a youngster comes in, there is a little excitement, and that excitement is visible.”
