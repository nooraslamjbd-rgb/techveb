## Some of the speakers founders will hear from in Boston

### Steve Fredette on building a company that keeps reinventing itself 

Boston founders do not have to look far for an example of what can happen when a startup finds its market and continues evolving with its customers. 

[Steve Fredette](https://www.linkedin.com/in/stevefredette/), president, co-founder, and director of [Toast](https://pos.toasttab.com/), takes the Impact Stage for “Building a Company That Keeps Reinventing Itself.” 

Toast’s story offers founders a compelling case study in identifying a customer problem, finding product-market fit, winning customers, and ultimately scaling a Boston startup into a public company. 

But reaching scale does not mean the work is finished. Fredette’s conversation will look at what it takes to continue evolving a company as customer expectations, technology, and the market change around it, while staying connected to the needs that helped create the business in the first place. 

### Kent Bennett on knowing when product-market fit is real 

Growth can be exciting. Growing too early can be expensive. 

[Kent Bennett](https://linkedin.com/in/kentbennett), partner at [Bessemer Venture Partners](https://www.bvp.com/), will lead “Finding Product Market Fit Before You Scale,” focusing on one of the most important questions an early-stage founder can answer: Are customers actually telling you that you are ready to grow? 

The session will give founders a chance to think more critically about the signals that matter before adding headcount, increasing spend, or accelerating go-to-market efforts. 

It is a useful counterpoint to startup culture’s constant pressure to move faster. Sometimes the smartest next move is making sure the foundation is actually there.

### Rudina Seseri on when to scale and when to hit the brakes 

That same tension takes center stage with [Rudina Seseri](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rudinaseseri/), founder and managing partner of [Glasswing Ventures](https://glasswing.vc/). 

In “When to Scale and When to Hit the Brakes,” Seseri will explore how founders can distinguish between the moments that call for acceleration and the moments when restraint may protect the business. 

Founders are constantly making trade-offs between growth and discipline. Knowing when not to scale can be just as consequential as knowing when to go all in. 

### Lior Div on building for AI from day one 

AI is reshaping how new companies think about products, operations, and teams, but adding AI to an existing process is different from building a company around it from the start. 

[Lior Div](https://www.linkedin.com/in/liordiv/), CEO and co-founder of [7AI](https://7ai.com/company), will tackle that distinction in “Built for AI From Day One.” 

For founders building in the current technology environment, the conversation provides an opportunity to think beyond AI as a feature and toward how it can influence the architecture and operating model of an entire company. 

### Chase Garbarino on the Boston founder playbook 

The startup world does not begin and end in Silicon Valley. 

[Chase Garbarino](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chasegarbarino/), CEO and co-founder of [HqO](https://www.hqo.com/), will take the Impact Stage for “The Boston Founder Playbook,” examining what it means to build and scale a company from Boston. 

For founders across New England and the broader East Coast ecosystem, the conversation offers a particularly relevant perspective on talent, customers, capital, and community outside the Bay Area. 

Combined with Fredette’s Toast story, it also gives this year’s Summit a strong local through line: Ambitious companies can be built in Boston, and founders here do not need to copy Silicon Valley to do it. 

### Dave Balter on the pitch that gets you another meeting 

A pitch does not have to close the deal. It has to make someone want the next conversation. 

[Dave Balter](https://www.linkedin.com/in/davebalter/), venture partner at [True Ventures](https://www.trueventures.com/) and CEO of [Edisyl](https://edisyl.com/), will explore that idea in “Your Pitch Has to Earn the Next Conversation.” 

For founders accustomed to explaining their companies every day, the session is a reminder that knowing your business and communicating its story are two different skills. 

Later in the afternoon, attendees will see that theory put into practice.

## Watch founders pitch live 

At “So You Think You Can Pitch?,” founders will put their stories to the test live on the Build Stage. 

Balter will be joined by [Chirag Shah](https://www.linkedin.com/in/chiragative/), partner at [Define Ventures](https://www.definevc.com/), and [Simran Suri](https://www.linkedin.com/in/simran-suri-540745147/), principal at [Maveron](https://www.maveron.com/), as startups make their cases in front of an investor judging panel. 

For attendees, it is an opportunity to watch what happens when founders have limited time to make an idea connect, and to hear how investors react in real time. 

It is one of the most interactive parts of the Founder Summit program and a chance to learn from both the pitches that land and the ones that leave questions unanswered.
