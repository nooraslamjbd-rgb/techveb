## Key Takeaways

- English mayors will gain powers to implement an overnight tourist tax.
- The levy will be a percentage of accommodation costs rather than flat fees.
- Local leaders decide how revenue from the visitor tax is reinvested locally.
- Trade body UKHospitality warns the tax could add £120 to family holidays.
- Similar visitor levies already operate successfully across Europe and Scotland.

## New Powers for Regional Leaders

Mayors across England are set to receive new powers enabling them to introduce an overnight visitor levy on tourists. According to plans reported by BBC News, local leaders will be permitted to implement an uncapped percentage levy on hotels, bed and breakfasts, and alternative accommodations.

Unlike fixed daily charges, the proposed "tourist tax" will scale with accommodation pricing. Government officials believe this structure protects budget travelers while granting local authorities essential fiscal flexibility.

### Local Reinvestment Model

Under the proposed framework, individual regions will decide whether to enact the levy and how the resulting tax revenues should be reinvested. Ministers emphasize that local voters and leaders hold the authority to determine what best serves their regional economy.

## Hospitality Industry Raises Pushback

Trade body UKHospitality has voiced strong opposition to the measures, warning that the policy will place additional financial stress on domestic holidaymakers. Chief Executive Allen Simpson noted that an uncapped tax could add £100 to £120 to a standard family holiday in England.

### Economic Risks for Tourism

Industry leaders caution that local councils, facing tight budgets, may over-rely on the levy. Key industry concerns include:

* **Risk to Regional Jobs:** Higher total holiday costs could reduce overall domestic tourism.
* **Seasonal Impact:** Off-peak and shoulder-season operators may struggle to stay open.
* **Household Budgets:** Families holidaying within the UK face increased financial pinch.

Similar overnight visitor charges are already operational across major European destinations and parts of Scotland.
