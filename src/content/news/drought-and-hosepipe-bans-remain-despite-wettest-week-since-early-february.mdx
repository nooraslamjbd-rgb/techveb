## Ongoing Drought Conditions Across England and Wales

Despite experiencing the wettest week since early February, all of Wales and the majority of England remain under official drought status. According to recent data from the Environment Agency (EA), late August and early September rainfall has failed to offset months of prolonged dry weather.

### Reservoir Levels Continue to Decline

Figures released by the Environment Agency reveal that rainfall totals between late August and early September ranged from 26mm in south-east England to 48mm in central England. However, the National Drought Group highlighted that overall water storage in major reservoirs continues to fall.

Key observations from environmental authorities include:
* **Persistent Shortages:** Brief rain showers run off dry ground quickly without deeply penetrating aquifers.
* **Enforced Restrictions:** Temporary use bans remain enforced across ten water company territories.
* **Decreased Demand:** Cooler autumn temperatures have lowered domestic consumption, but normal levels are far off.

### Sustained Rainfall Required for Recovery

Water utility providers urge consumers to adhere to active hosepipe bans. While upcoming weather forecasts predict additional rainfall, the EA emphasizes that sustained, long-term precipitation throughout autumn and winter is necessary to normalize groundwater levels and safeguard supplies, as reported by BBC News.
