Massive landslides and flash floods on the border between **Nepal and Tibet** have caused widespread destruction and left **hundreds dead and thousands missing**. Search and rescue operations were under way for days, with Chinese state media reporting significant casualties and Nepalese officials warning of "huge scale loss".

Here is a clear summary of what is known so far, in our own words.

## What caused the deadly slide?

Nepal's foreign minister initially reported an **earthquake** on the Tibet–Nepal border shortly before the disaster, suggesting it may have triggered the floods. But the United States Geological Survey's analysis found **no earthquake** in the area, casting doubt on that explanation.

Instead, scientists increasingly point to a **high-altitude glacier or ice avalanche**. A large mass of ice and rock slipped into the river system near the Langtang range, briefly **damming the river before it gave way**, releasing a sudden and catastrophic wall of mud and water.

One possibility is that water from rainfall or melting ice destabilised the ground or ice and triggered a chain reaction. Climate change is known to be driving faster glacier melt across the Hindu Kush Himalaya, though scientists say it is too soon to pinpoint its precise role in this specific event. Researchers are now studying satellite images, glacial-lake records and rainfall data to reconstruct the order of events.

## How many people are dead or missing?

The number killed or missing has been **rising by the hour** as rescuers reach isolated valleys. Nepal suffered by far the greater human toll, while China's Tibet region also reported deaths and missing people.

According to Nepal's tourism authorities, **hundreds of foreign nationals were among the missing**, including many trekkers and pilgrims, with travellers from India, the United States, the United Kingdom, Ukraine, Malaysia and Canada among those reported caught up in the disaster.

## Which areas were affected?

The flood surged down the **Bhote Koshi** into the **Trishuli river system**, passing through the districts of **Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading** in central Nepal. The border area sits roughly 64 kilometres north of the capital, Kathmandu. Footage showed a wall of mud and rock sweeping away buildings, roads and vehicles along the Trishuli, and severe flooding was reported along the Bhote Koshi, Trishuli and Narayani rivers. Damage also occurred on the Tibetan side near **Gyirong County**.

## How are governments responding?

- **Nepal** declared the affected areas **disaster zones**, deployed its army and helicopters for search and rescue, and ordered emergency aid and free treatment.
- **China** called for an "all-out" search effort, promised stronger monitoring and early warning systems to prevent secondary impacts, and offered support to Nepal.
- **India** said it was prepared to provide Nepal with all possible humanitarian assistance.
- **Pakistan** and other countries also offered relief and expressed solidarity.

The top priority for authorities remained finding survivors and preventing further catastrophe from a **barrier lake** that formed upstream and briefly raised the risk of a second flood before being brought under control. As far as can be established, this remains one of the deadliest sudden disasters to strike the Nepal Himalaya in years.
