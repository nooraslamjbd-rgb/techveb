## Ofgem Announces 4% Energy Price Cap Increase

UK energy prices will hit a three-year high this autumn following a 4% increase in the price cap announced by regulator Ofgem. Starting in October, typical household gas and electricity bills will rise by approximately £60 annually, coinciding with peak winter demand.

According to BBC News, high international wholesale gas prices remain the primary driver behind the price cap adjustment.

## Impact on UK Households and Tariffs

While the rate hike affects millions on variable rates, over one-third of UK households currently hold fixed energy tariffs. These consumers will remain unaffected by the immediate price rise.

Key details of the price cap change include:
* **Typical Annual Rise:** Average bills will increase by £60 per year.
* **Wholesale Pressure:** Rising global gas costs continue to dictate UK domestic rates.
* **Winter Warning:** Energy consultancy Cornwall Insight predicts an additional 9% rise in early 2025.

## Relief Measures and Fixed Tariff Options

To mitigate consumer burden, the UK government is removing VAT from electricity bills starting in October. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh emphasized that ministers are actively exploring further protective measures for struggling families.

Neil Kenward, Ofgem's Director General for Markets, noted that consumers can still secure savings by switching to fixed tariffs, which currently offer rates £100 or more below the upcoming October cap.
