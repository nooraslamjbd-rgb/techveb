**If you were getting the impression that the saga over** [**Manchester City**](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city)**'s 115 charges was almost over, think again.**

Two years and 10 days since the hearing began, and 1,329 days after City were charged, we now know [a verdict has been reached, with the club guilty of the majority of financial rule breaches.](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/cr89jjwy4nqpo)

That is not because the Premier League or [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city) have told us - it is thanks to [the Athletic finding out and breaking the story., external](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7049721/2026/09/25/manchester-city-charges-verdict-premier-league/)

BBC Sport was later told the verdict was sent during the week to club executives, who had to sign a non-disclosure agreement to open the file.

A process can appear to be completely watertight, as this one certainly had been, but when the circle of trust is expanded, the chance of information getting out increases.

Despite this, public silence from the Premier League continues.

City did not address whether they had been found guilty in a statement making it clear - with an appeal expected - that the "process remains ongoing" from their point of view.

It all points to the suggestion that the long-awaited decision of the independent panel was not a case of this process nearing an end, but perhaps instead reaching the end of the beginning.

It appears the chaos and uncertainty is only going to increase now.

City fans, and the rest of football, are left to guess what the punishment might be. And when this all might reach a definitive conclusion.

The charges are so serious that some form of points deduction now appears inevitable, subject to the appeal by City, who have always insisted they have done nothing wrong.

When and how any punishment is applied will be crucial not just for City, but the Premier League as a whole.

And that's before we get to the prospect of clubs suing City for compensation.

Enzo Maresca's side go to [Liverpool](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/liverpool) on 11 October when the Premier League resumes after the international break. They could go six points clear at the top. But is that now rendered meaningless?

Let's look at possible scenarios - none of which are particularly appealing.

Deciding the punishment should be quicker than ascertaining guilt, but nothing is certain in this case.

As the charges have been denied, it will have to go to a sanction hearing convened by the independent panel.

BBC Sport has been told this hearing could still be months away, and the result may not be published until the appeal is completed.

At the hearing, everything will be on the table. Fines, points deductions and even demotion.

This differs from [Chelsea](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/chelsea)'s case earlier this year, as [the Stamford Bridge club self-reported and admitted guilt](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/c5ygz42gedno). That allowed them to negotiate with the Premier League and reach a sanctioning agreement.

It is possible that City could decide to hold their hands up and try to agree a deal - which would be better for the league - though that seems unlikely given the club's consistently strong denials over many years.

The panel will have to comb through the hundreds of pages of evidence and determine the punishment.

Remember, [Everton](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/everton) were docked six points, reduced from 10 on appeal, and then two points in separate cases for breaches of profit and sustainability rules (PSR). [Nottingham Forest](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/nottingham-forest) were deducted four points.

These were considered to be minor breaches, while City faced seven separate charges on PSR breaches in the Premier League alone, as well as a further five related to Uefa competitions.

This aspect alone suggests a large points penalty.

That is before you get into the inflated sponsorship revenue allegedly channelled through the companies' accounts as a means of meeting financial fair play rules, payments to players and managers, and a failure to comply with investigations.

Take all this into account, and it places an asterisk against the Premier League between 2009 and 2018.

[Chelsea](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/chelsea) and [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city), two of the competition's most successful clubs, have been part of that. While [Chelsea](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/chelsea) admitted effectively cheating, City have been found guilty. They got around the financial rules to put them in a position to be able to sign more expensive players than their finances should have permitted.

And if this season passes with no decision on the sanction while it is known City were guilty of the majority of counts, what does that mean for the integrity of the competition?

Could a penalty be backdated to this season when finally awarded? Unlikely.

So what if [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city) win the league?

Coventry are third from bottom right now. What if they finish in that position, are relegated, but would have stayed up had City's punishment come this season?

This inertia is terrible news for the Premier League as a competition.

There's a strong argument that a sanctioning agreement is the cleanest way out of this.

But that may not be palatable to the league as they would have to negotiate with City, who would presumably want a lesser punishment.

The usual process when any points penalty is handed down is that it is applied immediately, subject to appeal.

It could be a points deduction so large that it is effectively a relegation.

Alternatively, if City's membership of the Premier League was revoked, it is unclear how that might be applied mid-season.

It could also be that the punishment is a huge fine, although that would seem unlikely given points deductions to other clubs.

The question would be how soon we hear, and how long after the appeal is heard.

Usually, the league asks for an expedited process from the independent arbitration company which handles disciplinary cases.

But, as ever in this case, the complexities indicate this is not straightforward.

There is the chance, though slim, that the punishment is only applied to the league table after the appeal.

That would surely lead to protests from impacted teams this season, who will demand it should, as usual, come into effect immediately.

Premier League clubs are seeking legal advice about compensation from [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city) over the case.

One source, who held a senior executive position at two Premier League sides, said they expected clubs to submit individual compensation claims against [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city).

Another leading lawyer told the BBC that process could even be centralised by the league.

Premier League rules allow clubs to seek legal action against each other for compensation.

In June, [Everton](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/everton) were told they must pay Burnley £35m after they broke PSR over a three-year period.

Burnley argued the breach impacted their chances of staying in the Premier League, and sought compensation for the losses associated with being relegated.

[Everton](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/everton) are appealing, with sources previously saying they would "robustly and thoroughly" contest the ruling, but it provides an interesting backdrop to the [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city) case.

There is uncertainty over a statute of limitations, which would set a cut-off point for claims.

[Arsenal](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/arsenal), [Manchester United](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-united), [Liverpool](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/liverpool) and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/tottenham-hotspur) reportedly preserved their rights to claim compensation two years ago.

This is not just about [Manchester United](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-united) (twice) and [Liverpool](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/liverpool), who finished as runners-up to City in their title-winning seasons.

It is about those clubs who just missed out on the Champions League, or failed to make Europe, or even those who were relegated.

And it could even result in claims over the prize money for each Premier League place, or the potential commercial earnings lost by rival clubs.

Kieran Maguire, professor of football finance at the University of [Liverpool](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/liverpool), told BBC Sport it would be considered a "denial of an opportunity" by complainants.

Maguire explained that the "statute of limitations is not referenced" in the Premier League's traditional handbook, though that could be subject to a legal challenge from City.

But what of the compensation? Just looking at Champions League qualifying could bring huge financial claims.

"The Champions League in those days was worth a minimum of £30m," Maguire said. "I think by the time we got to 2018, it was probably worth £60m or £70m, and that's in prize money.

"On top of that, you could claim for loss of gate receipts, loss of sponsor bonuses. This is highly unlikely, but it's certainly being discussed.

"Players get bonuses for qualifying for the Champions League. Those bonuses, if you talk to people connected to the elite clubs, are normally in the region of 25%.

"An average salary in those days would probably, if you go back to 2012, be in the region of £80,000 or £90,000 a week. Twenty-five percent of that is four or five million pounds a year. That's how the issues stack up."

Points deductions, fines, and compensation claims.

This is an extremely messy situation for the Premier League, and it does not look like being resolved anytime soon.
