ISLAMABAD, (APP - UrduPoint / Pakistan Point News - 29th Aug, 2026) [Federal](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/board-of-education-federal.html) Minister for National Heritage and Culture, Aurangzeb Khan Khichi on Saturday said [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/pakistan/) and Uzbekistan shared much more than formal diplomatic relations, as their ties were rooted in shared history, culture and spiritual heritage.

Addressing a special cultural concert titled “Uzbekistan-Pakistan: Melodies of Friendship and Brotherhood” as chief guest at the Pakistan National Council of the Arts (PNCA), organized by the Embassy of Uzbekistan to mark the 35th Anniversary of the Independence of Uzbekistan, the federal minister said [poetry](https://www.urdupoint.com/poetry/), music, art and ideas had travelled across the region for centuries, bringing the two peoples closer.

The event was attended by a large number of diplomats, high officials and dignitaries of various countries.

The minister congratulated the Government and people of Uzbekistan on the 35th anniversary of its independence and said the theme of the evening, “Melodies of Friendship and Brotherhood,” beautifully captured the deep [bond](https://www.urdupoint.com/business/prize-bonds.html) between the two countries.

“Music needs no translation. It speaks directly to people and reminds us of how much we share,” the Minister said, adding that he was delighted to welcome the Uzbek artists who had come to Pakistan to showcase the richness of Uzbekistan’s culture and musical traditions.

He said Pakistan greatly valued its cultural partnership with Uzbekistan and expressed the hope that cooperation would be expanded in heritage, museums, literature, music, performing arts, traditional crafts and exhibitions.

He particularly emphasized creating more opportunities for young artists, writers and cultural professionals from both countries to meet, learn and work together.

The minister thanked the Embassy of Uzbekistan and Ambassador Alisher Tukhtaev for arranging the cultural evening and contributing to bringing the peoples of Pakistan and Uzbekistan closer.

In his remarks, Ambassador Alisher Tukhtaev said the concert was a “cultural gift from the people of Uzbekistan to the friendly people of Pakistan”, reflecting the deep-rooted friendship and growing partnership between the two brotherly countries. He highlighted the centuries-old links between the two societies through the Silk Road, which facilitated the exchange of knowledge, literature, science, music and art.

The Ambassador also recalled the shared legacy of Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur and highlighted recent initiatives strengthening bilateral people-to-people ties, including the naming of Tashkent Street in Islamabad, the ongoing Babur Park project, and sister-city relations between Samarkand and Islamabad and between Termez and [Peshawar](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/board-of-education-peshawar.html).

He noted the growing cultural, academic, educational and [sports](https://www.urdupoint.com/sports/) exchanges between the two countries, as well as initiatives to promote [urdu](https://www.urdupoint.com/urdu-news) language and scholarship in Uzbekistan. Referring to the Uzbek President’s state visit to Pakistan earlier this year, he said it had provided fresh impetus to cultural and humanitarian cooperation, including collaboration on the preservation and promotion of the heritage of the Great Baburid Dynasty.

Ambassador Tukhtaev announced that preparations were underway for the “Days of Culture of Uzbekistan in Pakistan” later this year, which would bring a large Uzbek cultural delegation to Pakistan to showcase the country’s historical heritage, traditional arts and contemporary cultural achievements.

He also highlighted improving connectivity between the two countries, saying six direct flights were currently operating weekly between Tashkent and [Lahore](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/board-of-education-lahore.html) and Islamabad, while direct flights between Tashkent and [Karachi](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/board-of-education-karachi.html) would commence from September 03.

He said such connectivity would further facilitate tourism, [education](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/), [business](https://www.urdupoint.com/business/prize-bonds.html), cultural exchanges and people-to-people contacts.

The cultural evening featured a captivating programme by distinguished Uzbek artists, who presented a vibrant blend of musical and traditional dance performances, showcasing the rich cultural heritage and artistic traditions of Uzbekistan. Melodious musical presentations, accompanied by colorful and graceful traditional dances, enthralled the audience and offered a glimpse into the diverse cultural traditions of the country.

Through the universal language of music and artistic expression, the performers beautifully celebrated the enduring [bonds](https://www.urdupoint.com/business/prize-bonds.html) of friendship, brotherhood and mutual respect between the peoples of Uzbekistan and Pakistan.

The performances highlighted the longstanding historical and cultural affinities between the two countries and underscored the role of cultural exchanges in bringing the two societies closer.

The evening concluded with expressions of friendship and goodwill between the two brotherly nations, with Ambassador Tukhtaev expressing confidence that cultural ties would continue to grow and contribute to stronger bilateral relations.
