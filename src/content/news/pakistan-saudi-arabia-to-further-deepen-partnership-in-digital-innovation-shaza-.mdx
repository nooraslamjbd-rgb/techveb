ISLAMABAD, (APP - UrduPoint / Pakistan Point News - 1st Sep, 2026) [Federal](https://www.urdupoint.com/education/board-of-education-federal.html) Minister for Information Technology and Telecommunication Shaza Fatima Khawaja on Tuesday reaffirmed Pakistan’s resolve to further deepen its partnership with the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, expressing confidence that continued collaboration in digital innovation, capacity-building and emerging technologies would open new avenues of shared growth and prosperity for both nations.

Addressing the LEAP 2026 conference in Riyadh, titled “How We Built a Digital Nation”, she underscored that [Pakistan](https://www.urdupoint.com/pakistan/) had anchored its digital transformation in legislation through the Digital Nation Pakistan Act, which established the National Digital Commission and Pakistan Digital Authority to ensure institutional ownership.

She further said the legal foundation had been complemented by investment in connectivity and competitiveness, including one of the largest spectrum (5G) auctions, the creation of Special Technology Zones and reduced taxation for the IT industry.

Shaza Fatima Khawaja highlighted that the digital transformation journey was guided by the vision of Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and supported by the Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC), chaired by the Prime Minister and Chief of Army Staff and Chief of Defence Forces, Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, with the participation of all provincial chief executives.

She added that, building on these foundations, Pakistan was now developing a national digital stack which, combined with a young workforce, political and economic stability, and expanding digital infrastructure, positioned the country as one of the region’s most attractive investment destinations.

The government’s approach to growth, she affirmed, remained anchored in innovation, partnerships and smart investment.

Sharing insights from Pakistan’s digital journey, the minister said that governments succeeded by leading through sound policymaking and strong institutions, thereby creating an enabling environment in which the private sector could take the lead in driving growth.

She emphasized that a dedicated central digital agency was essential to translating policy into effective execution, while discipline and measurable KPIs kept large-scale digital transformation focused and on track.

The minister further underscored that the state’s role lay in building core digital infrastructure that empowered the private sector to innovate and build upon it, noting that genuine digital progress was achieved through partnerships.

Earlier in the day, the minister formally inaugurated the Tech Destination Pakistan Pavilion, featuring 25 startups and 50 leading Pakistani technology companies showcasing innovative solutions in AI, automation and digital technologies.

Pakistan’s participation in LEAP 2026 aims to facilitate B2B partnerships, attract international investment and expand global market access for Pakistan’s technology sector, while strengthening international [business](https://www.urdupoint.com/business/prize-bonds.html) networks and creating new opportunities for technology exports.
