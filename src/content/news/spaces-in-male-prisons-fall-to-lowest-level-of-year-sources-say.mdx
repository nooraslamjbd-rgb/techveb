## UK Male Prison Estate Faces Severe Capacity Crunch

Available spaces across the male prison system in England and Wales have reached their lowest level this year, dropping to roughly 1,500 free cells as of September 1, according to sources reported by BBC News.

This capacity figure represents less than half of the 3,040 available spaces recorded by the Ministry of Justice (MoJ) in early June, underscoring systemic pressure on correctional facilities.

### Government Response and Capital Investment

To mitigate immediate overcrowding, the Ministry of Justice announced a £110 million plan funded through existing departmental budgets. 

Key emergency actions include:
- Converting administrative rooms, laundry spaces, and storage areas into functional prison cells.
- Adjusting early release criteria to ensure serious offenders serve longer sentences.
- Reallocating existing funds to support critical physical capacity upgrades.

### Legislative Reforms to Indefinite Sentences

Alongside immediate space expansion, Justice Secretary Alex Norris outlined plans to introduce legislation ending Imprisonment for Public Protection (IPP) sentences before the end of the current Parliament. 

IPP sentences, which were previously used for high-risk offenders, held individuals indefinitely until the Parole Board determined they no longer posed a risk to public safety.
