## Ex-Border Patrol Chief Criticizes Minneapolis Withdrawal

Former U.S. Border Patrol Chief Greg Bovino has publicly criticized the Trump administration's decision to pull federal agents out of Minneapolis, describing the move as a surrender. Speaking to BBC News, Bovino expressed regret that enforcement actions were scaled back, claiming deportation efforts have now hit a virtual standstill.

Bovino was removed from his position earlier this year following the fatal shootings of U.S. citizens Renee Good and Alex Pretti during a high-profile deportation operation in January.

### Backlash and Pending Lawsuits

The fatal incidents, captured on video, sparked nationwide public outrage and bipartisan criticism, leading President Donald Trump to order a de-escalation and pull hundreds of immigration agents from the city.

Following the events, Renee Good's family filed civil lawsuits against several top officials, including Bovino and agent Jonathan Ross. The lawsuits allege wrongful death and state-sponsored violence.

* Key Allegations: Lawsuits claim a conspiracy to violate civil rights during protests.
* Bovino's Response: Bovino dismissed the claims, noting that people do lawsuits all the time.
* White House Position: Administration representatives previously labeled the legal claims as baseless.

Bovino remains steadfast in calling for tougher border and immigration enforcement across major American cities.
