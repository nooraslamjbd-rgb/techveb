**Pep Guardiola is expected to attend** [**Manchester City**](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city)**'s Champions League tie against Paris St-Germain next week - the club's first home match since they were** [**found guilty**](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/c63reg93xwzro) **of the majority of the 115 charges brought against them by the Premier League.**

It will mark the first time the Spaniard has returned to Etihad Stadium since he left the club at the end of last season after a decade as manager.

Earlier this month, an independent commission found the club guilty of all charges relating to breaches of the Premier League's financial regulations between the 2009-10 and 2017-18 seasons.

City continue to deny any wrongdoing [and have lodged an appeal](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/articles/cqn7470513n0o) against the findings in the Premier League's report that stated the club falsely inflated commercial revenues by £830m through "sham" contracts.

In the aftermath of the verdict, 55-year-old Guardiola backed City's owners and leadership and says he will "always" be behind the club.

"I want every single [Manchester City](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/football/teams/manchester-city) fan across the world to know I am here; more than ever," he posted on social media.

"I am, always have been, always will, be behind my club, my owner, my chairman, Ferran \[Soriano\], the players, staff, Enzo \[Maresca\] and you, our unique supporters.

"Let me be clear: we will get through this together. I love you all."
