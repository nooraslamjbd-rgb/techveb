## The August 2026 floods: a quick orientation

Three flood stories defined late August 2026 in South and East Asia, and they are easy to confuse:

1. **Nepal (Bhote Koshi / Trishuli)** — the deadliest: a glacier- and ice-collapse flood on August 26 that killed more than 600 people with thousands missing.
2. **China (Tibet / Gyirong)** — the *same* border disaster on the Chinese side, plus **Typhoon Narra** and the **East Asia monsoon** causing separate, widespread flooding across southern China.
3. **Kashmir** — no fresh 2026 event, but serious underlying risk: the 2025 Kishtwar cloudburst and the retreating glaciers it shares with the rest of the Himalaya.

## The core facts, in brief

The Nepal disaster began with a high-altitude glacier collapse near the Langtang range. Ice and rock dammed the Bhote Koshi, then broke, sending a wall of mud and water down through Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading. Because the trigger was ice and rock rather than rain, warnings were nearly impossible to send in time. Around 19 bridges, 40 kilometres of road and six hydropower facilities were destroyed. A barrier lake that formed upstream was drained to prevent a second flood.

On the Tibetan side, Gyirong County suffered a mudslide from the same event, with China reporting deaths and hundreds missing. Meanwhile Typhoon Narra and the monsoon flooded populous southern provinces, and China faced criticism over the limited transparency of its Tibet response.

For Kashmir, the honest answer is that there was no fresh 2026 flood like Nepal's — but the valley sits in the same warming mountain belt and must plan for both cloudburst-driven and glacier-driven floods.
