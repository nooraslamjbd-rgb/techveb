## Pakistan Extends Diplomatic Greetings to North Korea

In a notable diplomatic gesture, Pakistan officially extended greetings to North Korea on the 78th anniversary of the country’s founding. President Asif Ali Zardari sent a formal letter to DPRK President Kim Jong Un, highlighting cordial bilateral relations built on goodwill.

### Public Exchange Amid Strained Relations

Alongside President Zardari's message, Pakistan’s Foreign Office published a public statement extending warm felicitations to Pyongyang. As reported by Dawn News, such public exchanges have become rare in recent years due to international constraints and geopolitical sanctions.

Foreign Office spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan clarified the gesture, stating that exchanging felicitation messages is a standard diplomatic practice.

### Historical Context and UNSC Sanctions Compliance

Pakistan and North Korea established consular relations in 1968, upgrading to full ambassadorial status in 1972. However, bilateral engagement has remained low-key for over two decades due to international sanctions targeting Pyongyang's nuclear program.

Key aspects of the current relationship include:

- **Embassy Status**: Pakistan’s mission in Pyongyang is currently non-operational, while North Korea continues to operate its embassy in Islamabad.
- **Sanctions Enforcements**: Islamabad strictly enforces UNSC restrictions, including commercial trade limitations, flight suspensions, and stringent diplomatic visa requirements.
