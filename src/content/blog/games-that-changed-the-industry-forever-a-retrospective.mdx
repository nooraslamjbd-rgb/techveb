<h2>Moments That Defined Gaming</h2><p>Certain games don't just entertain--they fundamentally change what games can be. These industry-defining titles introduced mechanics, technologies, or design philosophies that permanently altered the gaming landscape.</p><h2>Super Mario Bros. (1985)</h2><p>Mario didn't invent the platformer, but it perfected the formula so thoroughly that it defined a genre and saved the gaming industry after the 1983 crash. Tight controls, imaginative level design, and a sense of discovery set the template for every platformer that followed.</p><h2>Doom (1993)</h2><p>Doom popularized the FPS genre, pioneered modding culture with WAD files, and popularized networked multiplayer. Its shareware distribution model proved that giving away the beginning of a game could sell the rest. The id Tech engine became the foundation for countless games.</p><h2>World of Warcraft (2004)</h2><p>WoW didn't create the MMO, but it made the genre mainstream. At its peak, 12 million subscribers proved that online gaming could be a mass-market phenomenon. Its design influenced everything from loot systems to quest design across all genres.</p><h2>Minecraft (2011)</h2><p>Minecraft proved that graphical fidelity doesn't define success. Its procedural generation, crafting systems, and creative mode spawned an entirely new genre of survival-crafting games and demonstrated the power of user-generated content.</p><h2>Fortnite (2017)</h2><p>Fortnite Battle Royale popularized the battle royale genre, pioneered the battle pass monetization model, and became a cultural phenomenon that transcended gaming. Its influence on gaming business models and social features continues to shape the industry.</p>
