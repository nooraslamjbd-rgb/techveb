faq:
  - question: "How does CRISPR gene therapy work?"
    answer: "CRISPR uses a guide RNA to direct a bacterial enzyme like Cas9 to specific DNA sequences, cutting the DNA to disable or repair targeted genes."
  - question: "What medical conditions can CRISPR treat today?"
    answer: "CRISPR treatments are approved for sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia, with active clinical trials targeting cancer and inherited blindness."
  - question: "What is the primary ethical concern regarding CRISPR?"
    answer: "The main ethical concerns center on human germline editing, which introduces inheritable genetic changes, alongside high treatment costs and unequal patient access."
  - question: "What is the difference between traditional CRISPR-Cas9 and base editing?"
    answer: "Standard CRISPR-Cas9 cuts double-stranded DNA to make edits, whereas base editing changes individual DNA base letters directly without breaking the double helix."
