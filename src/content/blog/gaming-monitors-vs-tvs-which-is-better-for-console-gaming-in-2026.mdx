<h2>The Great Debate</h2><p>Console gamers in 2026 face a growing choice between gaming monitors and TVs. Both options have improved dramatically, but they serve different use cases. Here's how to decide which is right for your setup.</p><h2>TV Advantages</h2><p>Modern OLED TVs like the LG C4 and Samsung S95D offer larger screen sizes (55-77 inches), stunning HDR performance, and immersive gaming from a couch. HDMI 2.1 support enables 4K/120Hz gaming, VRR, and ALLM for PS5 and Xbox Series X.</p><h2>Monitor Advantages</h2><p>Gaming monitors offer higher refresh rates (up to 540Hz), lower input lag, and faster response times. They're ideal for desk setups, competitive gaming, and situations where you sit close to the screen. Size-for-size, monitors typically cost less than equivalent TV technology.</p><h2>Input Lag and Response Time</h2><p>Modern OLED TVs have reduced input lag to under 10ms at 4K/120Hz, closing the gap with monitors. However, high-end gaming monitors still offer 1ms or lower response times, which matters for competitive gaming. For most console gamers, TV input lag is no longer a significant concern.</p><h2>The Hybrid Approach</h2><p>Many gamers are adopting a dual-display setup: a large OLED TV for single-player cinematic experiences and a gaming monitor for competitive play. This approach provides the best of both worlds but requires the space and budget for two displays.</p><h2>Recommendations</h2><p>For PS5/Xbox owners gaming primarily from a couch, a 55-65 inch OLED TV is ideal. For desk setups or competitive gaming, a 27-32 inch gaming monitor with high refresh rate is the better choice. PC gamers who also use their system for work may prefer a versatile high-refresh monitor.</p>
