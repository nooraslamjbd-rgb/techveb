Global logistics company CEVA Logistics is continuing to restore systems after a cyberattack disrupted operations at eight warehouses across Europe. The incident highlights the growing vulnerability of logistics and supply chain infrastructure to cyber threats.

## The Attack

The cyberattack targeted CEVA Logistics, a major global freight and logistics company that operates across 160 countries. The incident affected warehouse operations at eight facilities in Europe, disrupting the flow of goods and creating ripple effects across supply chains that depend on CEVA's services.

While the full details of the attack are still being investigated, the disruption at multiple facilities suggests a coordinated attack or a systemic compromise that spread across the company's network. The attack follows a pattern of increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks targeting logistics companies.

CEVA Logistics has been working to restore affected systems and resume normal operations. The company has engaged cybersecurity firms to investigate the incident and strengthen its defenses against future attacks.

## The Growing Threat to Logistics

The CEVA attack is part of a broader trend of cybercriminals targeting logistics and supply chain companies. These organizations are attractive targets because they sit at critical nodes in global commerce, and disruption to their operations can have cascading effects across multiple industries.

Recent years have seen a series of high-profile cyberattacks affecting logistics companies. The 2021 Colonial Pipeline attack, while targeting energy infrastructure, demonstrated the potential for cyberattacks to disrupt critical supply chains. More recently, attacks on freight platforms, shipping companies, and warehouse management systems have become increasingly common.

The logistics industry faces particular challenges in cybersecurity. Many logistics companies operate legacy systems that were not designed with modern cyber threats in mind. Additionally, the need for real-time coordination between multiple parties creates numerous potential entry points for attackers.

## Impact on Supply Chains

The disruption at CEVA's warehouses has implications beyond the company itself. CEVA handles logistics for major retailers, manufacturers, and e-commerce companies, meaning that disruptions to its operations can affect the availability of products across multiple markets.

Supply chain disruptions caused by cyberattacks are particularly challenging because they combine the technical complexity of cybersecurity incidents with the operational complexity of logistics. Restoring warehouse operations requires not just fixing the technical compromise but also dealing with the backlog of goods that accumulated during the disruption.

For businesses that depend on third-party logistics providers, the CEVA incident underscores the importance of supply chain resilience. Companies should assess the cybersecurity posture of their logistics partners and develop contingency plans for disruptions.

## What Organizations Should Do

The CEVA attack offers several lessons for organizations across industries. First, supply chain cybersecurity should be treated as a critical business risk, not just an IT concern. Organizations should assess and monitor the cybersecurity practices of their key suppliers and partners.

Second, incident response planning should account for supply chain disruptions. Having backup logistics providers and alternative supply routes can help organizations maintain operations when a key partner is affected.

Third, basic cybersecurity hygiene remains essential. Many logistics attacks exploit known vulnerabilities that could have been patched. Regular security updates, access controls, and employee training can prevent many attacks.
