<h2>Your Body Is Your Most Important Peripheral</h2><p>Long gaming sessions can take a serious toll on your body. Repetitive strain injuries, back pain, eye strain, and carpal tunnel syndrome are common among gamers who neglect ergonomics. Setting up your gaming space properly can prevent injuries and actually improve your performance.</p><h2>Monitor Position</h2><p>Position your monitor at arm's length with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. The center of the screen should be about 15-20 degrees below your natural eye line. This reduces neck strain and eye fatigue during long sessions.</p><h2>Chair and Desk Setup</h2><p>Your gaming chair should support your lumbar curve with your feet flat on the floor and knees at 90 degrees. Your desk height should allow your elbows to rest at 90 degrees with your forearms parallel to the floor. Standing desk converters offer the flexibility to alternate between sitting and standing.</p><h2>Hand and Wrist Care</h2><p>Take 5-minute breaks every hour to stretch your hands and wrists. Wrist rests can help maintain neutral wrist position, but the most important factor is taking regular breaks. Many esports professionals now incorporate hand stretching routines into their training.</p><h2>Eye Health</h2><p>Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Enable blue light filters on your monitors and consider anti-glare screen protectors. Proper room lighting that matches your screen brightness reduces eye strain significantly.</p>
