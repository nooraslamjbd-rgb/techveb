## Key Takeaways

- OLED wins for contrast and dark-room movies; bright Mini-LED QLED wins for bright living rooms and static content.
- Size should be chosen by viewing distance — 65\" for ~8-10 ft, 75\" for ~10-12 ft.

The best TV in 2026 is the one matched to your room, your viewing habits and the size your couch allows. Prices and ratings below are indicative editorial picks.

## OLED vs QLED vs Mini-LED: the short version

- **OLED** — each pixel lights itself, giving **true blacks**, stunning **contrast** and wide viewing angles. Best in **dark rooms** for movies. Costs more and should be treated carefully with static content.
- **QLED** — a bright LCD with quantum-dot colour. Great in **bright rooms**, no burn-in concern, generally cheaper, but blacks and viewing angles are weaker than OLED.
- **Mini-LED QLED** — QLED with many more LEDs for **fine local dimming**. Narrows the contrast gap versus OLED while keeping high brightness and comfort with static content. Often the best value in 2026.

## The picks, ranked

### 1. Best cinema: premium OLED

For dark-room viewing, a **premium OLED** with **perfect blacks and superb HDR** is the benchmark. Wide viewing angles and per-pixel control make movies and games look spectacular. If you mostly watch at night in a dim room, this is the pick.

### 2. Best all-round / bright room: Mini-LED QLED

For most living rooms, a **bright Mini-LED QLED** is the smart choice. It produces **excellent HDR peak brightness**, holds up in daylight and spares you burn-in worries with news channels and sports tickers — while costing less than OLED in larger sizes.

### 3. Best budget: 4K QLED

A **55-inch 4K QLED** with decent colour and HDR is a dependable, affordable everyday set. It won't match the flagships on contrast or brightness, but for a family living room it gets the job done at a fraction of the price.

### 4. Best gaming: high-refresh with HDMI 2.1

Gamers should prioritise a panel with **120Hz+**, **low input lag** and **full HDMI 2.1 bandwidth** with **VRR**. Both OLED and Mini-LED sets in this class are excellent; choose OLED for best contrast in a dark game room, Mini-LED for a bright one.

## Choosing the right size

Use your **viewing distance** rather than room size:

- **55-inch** — from about 7-9 feet.
- **65-inch** — from about 8-10 feet.
- **75-inch and up** — from about 10-12 feet or more.

Bigger generally feels more cinematic, but only if you can feed the panel and sit far enough back to see the whole screen comfortably.

## Buying checklist

- **Match tech to lighting**: OLED for dark rooms, Mini-LED/QLED for bright rooms.
- **Confirm HDMI 2.1** if you game — check which ports support 120Hz and VRR at 4K.
- **Skip 8K** unless you need it; 4K is the value sweet spot in 2026.
- **Treat OLED with care** around static logos and UI if you watch lots of fixed content.
